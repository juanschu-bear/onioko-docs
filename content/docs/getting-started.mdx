<Callout type="warn" title="Current state">
  There is no self-service key and no public sandbox yet. Access to the ONIOKO API is granted during a controlled onboarding, against a real integration. The [status page](./status) shows which resources are released.
</Callout>

## How access works

1. **Conversation first.** You describe which system ONIOKO should sit in (robot, avatar, practice system, platform) and whether compute may leave the device. The matching [flow chart](./flow-charts) becomes the shared picture.
2. **Scope and contract.** Purpose, data location, retention and deletion deadlines are fixed in the [integration contract](./integration-contract) before any key exists.
3. **Controlled documentation.** You receive the API reference, SDK and test access for the released resources. The same content appears here as soon as a resource is released publicly.
4. **First session against your system.** Not against a demo. The first thing you see is a real signal from your own input, or an honest "unavailable".

## What to prepare

- The **consent flow** on your side: how a person agrees, how they see that perception is active, how they revoke.
- The **subject mapping**: how your identities map to pseudonymous subjects, if you want ONIOKO to know nothing about real identities.
- The **moments**: which situations in your product should be perceived at all. ONIOKO never records continuously.
- Your **deletion trigger**: what in your product starts a deletion request, and where the receipt goes.

## What the first 15 minutes will look like

Once Sessions, Perception and Consent are released, the minimal path is always the same, in every SDK:

```text
1. create subject            → a stable, pseudonymous reference
2. set consent               → purpose, scope, duration
3. create session            → bound to that subject
4. submit one input          → audio, video or a stream
5. read signals              → measured values, or "unavailable"
6. close session
```

Exact calls, fields and limits appear in the [API reference](./api-reference) the moment they are released. This page will then show the same six steps as runnable code.
